
GROWTH EXPERIMENTATION SQUAD · LYSSNA 5-SECOND TEST
Does the "Have Better Discussions" payoff screen land in 2 seconds?
Results summary — [Growth Exp] JTBD Payoff v1 Q3'26 · 2-Second Test

1. Overview
What was tested. Participants were placed in a scenario: they're preparing for a book club discussion of The Great Gatsby and want the conversation to be better than usual. After telling SuperSummary that what they need help with is to "Have better discussions about it," they were shown a payoff screen for 2 seconds and asked what they took away from it.
Recreation of the layout and copy shown for 2 seconds (not the original artwork).
Questions asked after the 2-second exposure:
2.1 — "In one sentence, what was that screen telling you?" (open text, required)
2.2 — "Based on what you just saw, what specifically would SuperSummary help you do?" (open text, required)
2.3 — "How clear was it what you were being offered?" (1–5 scale, required)
2.4 — "How much did that screen feel like it was about what you said you wanted?" (1–5 scale, required)

5. Key findings
The repositioning headline is only partly cutting through. "Way more than a summary" was echoed clearly by 36% of viewers, but a comparable 20% inverted it — walking away describing SuperSummary as a plain summarizer. The word "summary" appears sticky enough to dominate a 2-second glance over the "more than" qualifier meant to reframe it.
The specific job being tested does land for most people. 52% of respondents named a discussion-related benefit unprompted when asked what the product would help them do — a good signal that "Spark strong discussions" is relevant and legible for users who explicitly said they want "better discussions."
A meaningful share found 2 seconds too short to process the page. Around 1 in 5 responses across both open questions explicitly flagged running out of time or only registering "blocks" without retaining their content — plausible given the page carries a headline, subhead, and three feature cards at once.
The audio/"Listen on the go" card is nearly invisible. It drew no more than one loose mention across 25 responses, versus meaningful recall for the discussion and memory-refresh cards.
Clarity and relevance scores are moderate, not strong. Mean clarity is 3.36/5 and mean relevance-to-stated-want is 3.56/5; roughly half the sample scored 4–5 on each, while 12–20% scored low and, on relevance, over a third were neutral (3/5).
6. Recommendations
Test a headline that leads with the discussion benefit rather than opening on the word "summary," to reduce the share of viewers who read the message as "just a summary tool."
Lead with (or isolate) the "Spark strong discussions" card for users who have stated this specific job, instead of a generic three-card sequence, so the most job-relevant content isn't competing for a share of a 2-second glance.
Re-evaluate the "Listen on the go" card on this variant. It earned no meaningful recall here and may be diluting attention from the two cards that matter more to this segment.
Treat these scores as a floor, not a verdict. With ~1 in 5 participants saying the exposure felt too short, pair this test with a longer-exposure or live-traffic read (where users can scroll and read normally) before deciding whether the messaging itself needs to change versus simply needing more reading time.
Run a follow-up single-message variant — one clear benefit, fewer competing cards — to see whether it improves both clarity and discussion-specific recall relative to this three-card version.
