Executive summary
August 2026 was the largest evidence base the team has assembled in a single cycle. Read together, the
sources point to one dominant story and three supporting ones.

1. Billing clarity is the biggest lever we have. Nearly half of all cancellations (45%) happen within seven
days of subscribing, and a further 28% happen at annual renewal. The two moments share a root cause: users
do not understand what they are buying or when they will be charged. “7 days free” is read as a free trial but
works as a refund window; annual renewals arrive without warning; the Essentials/Plus split and the
annual-by-default selector produce mistaken purchases. Insights 1, 2 and 8 describe this problem from three
angles. Together they account for roughly 40% of stated cancel reasons, the majority of support volume, and
the only recurring detractor signal in NPS.

2. Usage is episodic — and our model is not. “I only needed SuperSummary for a short time” is the single
largest cancel reason (38%) and these users report the highest likelihood of returning. Students and parents,
who make up half of cancellations and carry the weakest NPS, embody this pattern. Insights 3 and 6 argue for
products built around the real usage cycle (single-guide passes, pause, easy reactivation) rather than for
retaining users who have no continuous need.

3. Book Club Members are the healthy core. BCM NPS is 68.5, “book club preparation” is the dominant
promoter theme every cycle, and the reference-layer features (Character List, Story Recap, Author Insights)
show strong product-market fit. Insights 4, 5, 9 and 10 describe where to invest in content depth, catalog, the
Book Hub and the Book Clubs product for that core.

4. Mobile Web is the weakest surface. NPS on Mobile Web fell to 42.4 with a 20% detractor share — double
Desktop and nearly four times the app. Insight 7 flags it as a platform risk that deserves a dedicated audit.

Beyond the previous review (Part 2). A second pass on the raw data surfaced seven findings that the earlier
key insights did not cover: a third of “cancellations” happen within 24 hours of purchase and are largely
defensive renewal opt-outs; renewal churn is the least recoverable and turns into chargebacks; failed-payment
emails trigger cancellation rather than recovery; login and identity friction quietly drives refunds; the cancel flow
is blind to avid readers; a third of users find the discussion questions themselves lacking; and several research
instruments cannot explain our weakest segments.

Reading across the insights for Q4
The seventeen insights cluster into five decisions leadership can take into planning.

Fix billing clarity first (Insights 1, 2, 8, 12)
Trial semantics, renewal notices and plan selection are the same problem seen from three angles. Together
they explain roughly 40% of stated cancel reasons and most support volume, and they are largely copy, email
and page-structure changes. Expected effects: fewer refunds and chargebacks, lower support cost, fewer
zero-score detractors, and cleaner data on why people actually leave.

Design for episodic use (Insights 3, 6)
A single-guide pass, a pause option and easy reactivation would serve the largest leaver cohort — and the
audience with the weakest NPS — better than annual-first retention. This is a business-model conversation as
much as a product one, and it should be tested before being assumed.

Invest in the core for Book Club Members (Insights 4, 5, 9, 10, 16)
Editorial quality and citations, catalog expansion with a visible request queue, a Book Hub built on the
reference layer, and Book Clubs tooling that makes discussion prep and reactions easy. These are the
features promoters already credit and the ones the PMF and prioritization data rank highest.

Fix the plumbing behind retention (Insights 11–15, 17)
Separate renewal opt-out from churn in the metrics, add a non-renewing option and pre-renewal notice,
redesign dunning and the cancel flow around stated reasons, unify identity and self-serve cancellation, and
repair the MBG form. These are lower-visibility changes than new features, but together they address the
tickets, refunds and disputes that currently absorb support capacity and mask the real churn rate.

Treat Mobile Web as a platform risk (Insight 7)
Audit access and entitlement on Mobile Web, reduce promotional density for search-arriving readers, and
instrument detractor follow-up so the next cycle can explain the score.

Method and caveats
• Chargebee reasons are the user’s stated selection in the cancellation flow; tenure is computed from

• Sprig exit-intent and intent surveys have small samples (n=3–44) and are used as qualitative signal, not as

• NPS figures are taken from the August 2026 NPS Continuous Discovery Report (550 validated
