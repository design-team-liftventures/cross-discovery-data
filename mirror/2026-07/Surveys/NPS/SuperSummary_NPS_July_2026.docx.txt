SuperSummary — NPS Continuous Discovery Report
July 2026 Cycle
Prepared by the Design Team, Lift Ventures  ·  Source: Sprig MCP (Production, BWedE9AHIM)  ·  Period: July 1–31, 2026
Executive Summary
The July 2026 cycle draws on 469 validated unique respondents across the four active in-product Sprig NPS surveys (BCM and Non-BCM segments on Website and Mobile App). Overall NPS for July is 65.9, up 2.5 points from June (63.4) and 7.0 points below the December 2025 program baseline of 72.9. The gain comes from a reduction in detractors rather than from promoter growth: the detractor share fell from 12.0% to 9.0% while the promoter share held roughly flat (75.4% to 74.8%) and the passive share rose from 12.6% to 16.2%.
Two structural patterns that held across prior cycles reversed this month. Desktop overtook Mobile App as the strongest platform (71.6 vs. 69.0), the first such inversion in the program, driven by a 12.7-point Mobile App decline. At the same time Mobile Web vacated the floor position, rising 16.3 points to 54.0, and the Non-BCM × Mobile Web cross-segment tripled from 12.8 to 40.0. The lowest reading in the cycle is now defined by audience rather than platform: Non-BCM / student at 38.2 (n=34, 26.5% detractors).

Key Findings
Overall NPS rose to 65.9 (+2.5 vs. June), and the gain is detractor-driven. The detractor share fell from 12.0% to 9.0% while promoters held flat and passives rose to 16.2%. The score remains 7.0 points below the December 2025 baseline of 72.9.
Desktop overtook Mobile App as the strongest platform (71.6 vs. 69.0). Mobile App fell 12.7 points from June (81.7), the largest single-segment movement in the cycle and the first reversal of the app-leads-platform pattern in the program. Mobile App carries the largest respondent base (n=197), so this movement drives the composition of the overall score.
Mobile Web vacated the floor position it held across every prior cycle. Mobile Web rose 16.3 points to 54.0, and Non-BCM × Mobile Web — the tracked high-risk cross-segment — tripled from 12.8 to 40.0. It is still the weakest cross-segment at 21.8% detractors, but no longer a negative or near-zero reading.
The audience gap narrowed to 12.0 points (BCM 69.8 vs. Non-BCM 57.8), the tightest of the tracked cycles. Within Non-BCM the spread is wide: librarian 87.5 (n=8) and teacher 71.1 (n=38) sit above the overall score, while student 38.2 (n=34) is the lowest reading in the cycle at 26.5% detractors.
annual_essentials now outperforms annual_plus (67.6 vs. 64.4), inverting the March 2026 pattern. In March, annual_plus led annual_essentials by 16.8 points. The reversal coincides with seven requests for deeper critical analysis, indicating that the premium tier is not currently carrying a clear perceived value increment.
Billing and cancellation remains the only established detractor theme (4 Non-BCM mentions, 1 BCM). No BCM detractor theme reached the three-mention threshold, because only 7 of 21 BCM detractors left free text. Root-cause analysis for the Mobile App decline is not recoverable from this instrument.
Month-over-Month Trend
July figures are computed from the current Sprig extraction. May and June values are carried forward from the prior cycle reports and were verified against the June build record. December 2025 (72.9) is the program baseline. Deltas show the June to July change.
Six of seven tracked metrics improved. Mobile App is the sole decline, and it moves in the opposite direction to Mobile Web, which gained 16.3 points in the same period. Unfound-Title Analysis (Q4)
13 of 469 respondents answered the unfound-title question (2.8%), against 14 of 374 (3.7%) in June. Of the 13, six named specific titles and seven gave non-specific or negative answers. Eight distinct titles were named, every one a single mention.
Low-volume flag. All eight titles are single mentions, so no title reaches a prioritisation threshold and this table cannot rank catalog additions. Three respondents gave directional rather than specific answers, which is more informative than the title list itself: "They are too numerous to list." [Score: 10, BCM]; "Yes several. It is usually newer books or ones less read by the best seller crowd." [Score: 10, BCM]; "some books in foreign languages." [Score: 10, Non-BCM]. Two of the eight named titles are Spanish-language, consistent with that last observation.
Capture remains recall-based and therefore thin. The standing recommendation to instrument unfound-title capture at the point of search, rather than through survey recall weeks later, carries forward unchanged and is the only route to usable demand data on this question.

Strategic Recommendations
Ranked by (1) number of respondents affected, (2) alignment with the BHAG (become as essential to reading as the book itself) and 3HAG (become the platform Book Club Members use daily), and (3) feasibility signal present in the data.
Diagnose the Mobile App decline with behavioural instrumentation, not survey data. Affects 197 respondents, the largest platform base, and represents a 12.7-point drop against a rising overall score. The available qualitative evidence is two navigation complaints, which is insufficient to establish cause. Because Mobile App is the surface on which the 3HAG depends, this ranks first despite the thin qualitative signal. Recommended next step: session replay and funnel analysis on app navigation paths for July, paired with a targeted in-app intercept for app detractors and passives. The high app passive share (19.8%) is the specific population to probe.
Close the billing and cancellation gap. The only established detractor theme, with four Non-BCM mentions and one BCM mention, and three of those respondents scored zero. Students carry 42.9% of Non-BCM detractors and the highest concentration of billing complaints. The failures described are operational rather than product-deep: cancellation not taking effect, charges without recognised subscription, and absent renewal notices. Feasibility is high — renewal reminders and a verifiable cancellation confirmation address most of the reported cases without a product build.
Build a premium analytical layer and re-establish the annual_plus value increment. Seven requests for deeper critical analysis constitute the largest request group, and annual_plus now trails annual_essentials by 3.2 points with a 20.0% passive share against 11.8%. The passive concentration on the premium tier indicates adequacy without differentiation, which is precisely the near-term commercial problem. Requests point to concrete content: stronger interpretive analysis, finer chapter breakdowns, more quoted material, and critical reception from external sources. Feasibility signal is strong because the requested increment is content depth, which is the established differentiator.
Ship a title-nomination mechanism and treat it as demand instrumentation. Six catalog requests, four catalog-gap mentions in detractor and passive text, and all Q4 evidence converge here, and one respondent explicitly asked whether a nomination path exists. Direct BHAG alignment: a reader cannot treat the platform as essential when the book in hand is missing. A nomination mechanism serves the request and simultaneously replaces recall-based Q4 capture with a continuous demand signal, making it the highest-leverage item relative to build cost.
Extend book club support from preparation into facilitation. Book club preparation is the dominant promoter theme at 29 mentions, and three requests ask for activities, games, and printable discussion cards — that is, tools for running a session rather than preparing for one. Four Non-BCM respondents also report book club use, indicating addressable demand outside the segment the BCM surveys capture. This is the strongest 3HAG alignment of any item and builds on demonstrated rather than hypothesised value.
Investigate the Non-BCM student segment. At 38.2 (n=34) with a 26.5% detractor share, students are the lowest cut in the cycle and the only segment in the red band alongside Non-BCM × Mobile Web. Their detractor text concentrates on billing rather than content, which suggests the problem is commercial mechanics rather than product fit. Sample size supports a directional read but not fine segmentation; a dedicated intercept is warranted.
Reduce sign-in and account-access friction. Three requests plus two passive mentions. Low volume, but the cost of the fix is low and access friction suppresses the daily-use behaviour the 3HAG requires. Ranked last on evidence volume, not on feasibility.
Research Gaps
Detractor free-text response rate. Only 13 of 42 detractors left free text (31.0%), and BCM detractors are the weakest case at 7 of 21. No BCM detractor theme reached the three-mention threshold, so the cycle cannot explain BCM dissatisfaction from this instrument. This has been a chronic limitation across cycles and is now the binding constraint on the most important open question in the report — the Mobile App decline. A dedicated detractor follow-up mechanism, triggered on submission of a score of 6 or below, would address it.
Unexplained platform divergence. Mobile App fell 12.7 points while Mobile Web rose 16.3 points in the same period. The two surfaces draw on separate surveys and separate respondent pools, and respondent volume rose 25.4% overall, so the divergence may reflect a change in who was sampled rather than a change in experience. Before acting on the app decline, verify that survey targeting, trigger events, and delivery volumes were unchanged between June and July.
Platform precision. iPad and some desktop Safari sessions report OS as "Mac OS", and iPad sessions using non-app browsers report as "iOS", so a share of both Desktop and Mobile Web is tablet traffic. July user-agent strings confirm iPad devices under both values. Tablet experience is therefore not separately measurable, despite plausibly differing from both phone and desktop.
Subscription plan coverage. Only 36.5% of respondents carry a non-legacy planId, up from 25.9% in June. The plus-versus-essentials inversion is the most commercially consequential finding in the cycle, and it rests on 158 annual-tier records. Migrating legacy plan identifiers would raise confidence in tier analysis materially.
Q4 capture. Unfound-title capture reached 2.8% of respondents with every named title at a single mention. The question cannot prioritise catalog investment in its current form. Point-of-search instrumentation remains the standing recommendation.
Perceived content provenance and quality. One Non-BCM respondent described the content as AI-written, and one BCM passive described it as general and repetitive with analysis missing. Both cut against the Trusted Expertise positioning and the depth-of-analysis promoter theme. Volume is far below the theme threshold, but the strategic exposure justifies a dedicated probe rather than waiting for the signal to accumulate.
Book club behaviour outside the BCM segment. Four Non-BCM respondents report book club use, and one Non-BCM-source respondent carries audienceCategory = book_club_member. The BCM survey constraint may under-capture book club behaviour, which would mean the segment central to the 3HAG is measured on an incomplete base.
Methodology
Data source. Sprig MCP against the Production environment (BWedE9AHIM). Survey existence and question identifiers were confirmed with get_surveys before extraction; question identifiers differ per survey, so open-text questions were matched by question text rather than by id. Responses were pulled with get_responses using with_meta and with_snapshots, paginated by cursor to exhaustion. Page size was capped at 100 for Website surveys and 70 for Mobile App surveys to stay within the 1MB tool limit; 120 and 150 both exceeded it. Visitor event arrays were discarded on ingest, retaining only attributes and meta.
Window. July 1, 2026 00:00:00 UTC inclusive to August 1, 2026 00:00:00 UTC exclusive, expressed as millisecond bounds [number] and [number].
Deduplication and validity. Sprig returns one row per question-answer. Rows were deduplicated on (responseGroupUid, question_id) and then collapsed to respondent level keyed on responseGroupUid, which is the unit of the validated count. Records were retained only where subscriptionDate is populated.
Score derivation. NPS = (% Promoters − % Detractors), with Promoters 9–10, Passives 7–8, and Detractors 0–6 derived from the raw 0–10 score on the NPS-type question. Sprig provides no pre-computed category field.
Classification. Audience was assigned by source survey (BCM versus Non-BCM), with audienceCategory used only as a cross-check. Platform was derived from meta.os and meta.browser: Mobile App where the OS contains Android or iOS and the browser contains WebKit or Chrome WebView; Mobile Web where the OS is mobile but the browser is not an app browser; Desktop otherwise. Subscriber classification follows the May 2026 rule, using status and subscriptionDate and disregarding isSubscribed.
Qualitative coding. Themes were coded from the "main reason for your score" question using raw text alongside the per-response Sprig theme labels, which arrive embedded on open-text rows and require no separate call. A theme requires three mentions across distinct respondents; anything below that is reported as an emerging signal. Every quotation is attributed with its respondent's raw NPS score and audience.
