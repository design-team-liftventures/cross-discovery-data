SuperSummary
CX Bug Analysis — July 2026
Continuous Discovery Cycle  |  Freshdesk support ticket export  |  Prepared by the Design Team, Lift Ventures
Executive Summary
This report analyses SuperSummary support tickets created between 1 and 30 July 2026 to identify technical defects that block users from completing intended actions. The source file contained 1,337 ticket records. Filtering proceeded in five stages: one record with an empty description was dropped; 411 records from the excluded payment and partner senders were removed; 23 records from automated system and no-reply addresses were removed; 91 support-agent outbound messages were removed; and a further 38 records comprising out-of-office autoresponders and vendor solicitations were removed. This left 749 user-originated tickets for classification.
Applying the two-signal bug test, which requires both a described technical failure and a condition that blocks the user from completing an intended action, produced 194 confirmed bug tickets from 181 distinct users. Bugs account for 25.9 per cent of user-originated contact in July.
The 194 tickets group into 17 themes. Eleven themes carrying 166 tickets classify as Critical, five themes carrying 27 tickets classify as Major, and one theme carrying a single ticket classifies as Minor. Billing and subscription-lifecycle defects account for 74 tickets. Account-access defects account for a further 68.
The dominant July finding is new. Twenty-six tickets report signing up for an advertised seven-day free trial and being charged immediately. One ticket reproduces a support-chat reply stating that SuperSummary does not offer a free trial, and a second ticket asks that the webpage be corrected because the offer is a seven-day refund window rather than a trial. The signup surface therefore presents a trial that the billing system does not honour. Under the severity taxonomy, a charge inside a stated free-trial window is always Critical.
Key Findings
1. A free-trial promise on the signup surface is not honoured by billing. Twenty-six tickets from 24 users describe an immediate charge after selecting a seven-day free trial, at $36, $60 and $9.95 price points. Supporting evidence inside the tickets indicates no trial exists in the billing configuration, which makes this a mismatch between the acquisition surface and the payment system rather than an isolated processing error. The pattern generates refund demands, chargeback threats and cancellations within minutes of purchase.
2. Password recovery is the single largest access failure. Twenty-eight tickets from 28 distinct users report that reset emails never arrive, that reset links fail when clicked, or that a password change cannot be completed. Several users confirm they checked spam folders. Because the reset path is the only recovery route for users who cannot sign in, each of these tickets also blocks subscription cancellation, which converts an access defect into a billing-retention risk.
3. Entitlement state does not reliably follow payment. Three themes describe the same underlying condition from different surfaces: 22 tickets where a paying subscriber cannot reach content, 17 tickets where the account page displays a free plan to an actively billed subscriber, and three tickets where a confirmed charge never activated a subscription. Combined, 42 tickets indicate that the payment event and the entitlement record diverge. PayPal, Apple Pay, card and Google sign-in populations all appear in this group.
4. Charges arrive without a locatable account. Nineteen tickets from 15 users report a charge they do not recognise, or a charge against an email address that has no account. Eight cite the $36 annual price and four cite the $9.95 monthly price. Three tickets from a single household describe a recurring $9.95 charge on a spouse’s card. This cluster carries legal exposure because four tickets use the language of fraud or dispute, and two state that a bank chargeback has already been initiated.
5. Post-cancellation billing has fallen sharply against the prior cycle. Charges after a confirmed cancellation account for 10 tickets in July, against 60 in May and 51 in April. The reduction is consistent with a fix having landed on the cancellation propagation path. The theme has not been eliminated and remains Critical, but it is no longer the leading billing defect. Cancellation friction has shifted upstream instead, into the 10 tickets where the cancel control is absent from the account page or the app.

Strategic Recommendations
1. Reconcile the free-trial offer with billing configuration. Twenty-six tickets and 24 users are affected, and the defect strikes at the moment of conversion. Either implement a genuine seven-day trial in the billing system or remove trial language from every acquisition surface and describe the seven-day refund window in its own terms. This is the highest-impact item because it converts new subscribers directly into refund requests and chargeback threats, damaging both revenue and payment-processor standing.
2. Repair the password recovery path end to end. Twenty-eight tickets make this the largest single theme. Instrument the reset pipeline for send, delivery and click, verify sender reputation and spam classification, and confirm the token remains valid when the link is opened. Because lockout also blocks cancellation, fixing recovery reduces both access complaints and billing disputes.
3. Make entitlement state a single source of truth across surfaces. Forty-two tickets across three themes trace to divergence between the payment event and the entitlement record. Add a reconciliation job that compares active charges in Chargebee against entitlement records, alerts on mismatch, and backfills. Prioritise the free-plan display case, which additionally hides the cancel control.
4. Unify identity so one subscription resolves across all sign-in methods. Eighteen tickets involve a paid entitlement attached to one identity while the user authenticates with another. Implement account linking across email, Google and Apple, apply case-insensitive email matching, and give support a merge tool. Apple private-relay addresses need explicit handling.
5. Surface cancellation on every platform and confirm it in writing. Ten tickets report the cancel control absent from the account page or app, and a further 10 report billing after cancellation. Expose cancellation on web and mobile, align help-centre instructions with the live interface, and send an immediate written confirmation. This reduces dispute volume and regulatory exposure.
6. Re-tune PDF anti-abuse thresholds and add a self-service appeal. Seven independent reports in one month indicate the threshold is misfiring on legitimate use, notably educators who print guides for classroom use. Raise or segment thresholds by plan and account age, replace the terms-of-service framing with a specific message, and provide a review request path rather than requiring a support ticket.
7. Close the orphaned-charge loop before disputes escalate. Nineteen tickets describe charges with no locatable account. Build a support-side lookup keyed on transaction identifier, card last-four and payment-processor reference so an agent can resolve a charge without an account match, and audit the signup flow for paths that create a subscription without an account record.
Research Gaps
Trial configuration. The ticket evidence indicates no free trial exists in billing while the signup surface advertises one. Direct inspection of the pricing page, the paid-acquisition creative and the Chargebee plan configuration would confirm where the mismatch originates and whether it was introduced by a recent change.
Reset email deliverability. Tickets establish that emails do not arrive but cannot distinguish a send failure from a spam-classification or reputation problem. Delivery telemetry by recipient domain would separate these, and the concentration of reports on major consumer domains suggests testing there first.
Entitlement divergence by payment method. PayPal, Apple Pay, card and Google sign-in all appear in the entitlement themes, but ticket volumes are too small to establish whether any method is disproportionately affected. A query comparing active charges to entitlement records, segmented by method, would settle the question raised in prior cycles.
Boundary between defect and repackaging. Seven tickets report upgrade prompts on features users believe their plan includes, at a time when plan contents appear to have changed. Plan-level entitlement records for these accounts would determine which reports are defects and which reflect intended gating that was not communicated.
Unreported incidence. Counts reflect tickets, not affected users. Users who abandon the product, resolve the problem themselves, or cannot reach the intake form are absent. Funnel instrumentation on the reset, checkout and cancellation paths would give incidence independent of contact volume.
Cancellation fix verification. Post-cancellation billing fell from 60 tickets in May to 10 in July. Confirming which change produced the reduction, and whether the 10 residual cases share a distinct cause, would establish whether the theme can be closed or requires further work.
Methodology
Source and scope.
The analysis used the Freshdesk export supplied for this cycle, covering 1,337 tickets created between 1 and 30 July 2026. Column names were mapped on load to the canonical schema of created time, email, subject and description. All rows were read; no sampling was applied.
Filtering.
Five stages ran in sequence. Records with an empty description were dropped. Records from the excluded senders, including the PayPal, Braintree, OpenPhone and TeachersPayTeachers addresses and the RocketMoney advocate domain, were removed. Automated system and no-reply senders were removed. Quoted reply history was then separated from user-authored text so that agent language quoted beneath a user message did not cause a genuine ticket to be discarded, and support-agent outbound messages were removed on the basis of the user-authored portion only. Out-of-office autoresponders and vendor solicitations were removed last. Curly punctuation was normalised before pattern matching, without which a substantial number of genuine reports fail to match on contractions.
Classification.
A ticket counts as a bug only where both required signals are present: a described technical failure, and a condition that blocks the user from completing the intended action. Single-signal tickets were excluded. Screening on failure vocabulary produced 256 candidates, each of which was then read individually. Sixty-two were reclassified as non-bugs and are listed in the accompanying exclusion file. Remaining tickets were assigned to exactly one theme under priority ordering, so a ticket describing several symptoms counts once against its closest theme. Severity followed the scope, compounding-harm and workaround criteria of the taxonomy; confidence followed the reproducibility criteria. Both were assigned at theme level from the evidence present in the constituent tickets.
Exclusion boundary.
Refund and cancellation requests that describe no technical failure were excluded, including auto-renewal surprises, dissatisfaction refunds and requests made under the money-back guarantee. This boundary is the largest single determinant of the reported bug count, and applying it differently moves the total materially. Two tickets reporting that a confusing plan selector led the user to choose an annual plan were also excluded, because the user completed the purchase and was not blocked; both are recorded as issues and warrant separate design attention.
Comparison basis.
Cross-month figures for April and May come from prior reporting cycles rather than from a re-run of this pipeline, and no June cycle was completed. May-to-July comparisons therefore skip an intervening month and should be read as directional. The July free-trial theme has no counterpart in prior cycles and appears to be new.
Limitations.
Counts measure reported contact rather than incidence. Single-best-fit assignment understates themes that frequently co-occur, most notably password recovery failure and cancellation failure, which appear together in a considerable number of tickets. Theme boundaries between an entitlement defect and an intended packaging change rest on judgement in a small number of cases, and those judgements are stated in the relevant theme rationales.
